## Question 1
What stood out to me most is that **small habits, repeated regularly, can change a lot**. Starting with something tiny makes it much easier to keep going.

## Question 2
**B = MAP** means that a behaviour happens when Motivation, Ability and a Prompt come together.

- **M – Motivation:** how much I want to do it.
- **A – Ability:** how easy it is to do.
- **P – Prompt:** the cue that reminds me to start.

Making a habit tiny lowers the effort, so it's easier to repeat.

## Question 3
Celebrating right after a habit gives me a **good feeling**. My brain links that feeling to the action, so I'm more likely to do it again.

## Question 4
The idea that stuck with me is getting just **1% better each day**. Those small gains add up to big progress over time.

## Question 5
*Atomic Habits* talks about **identity-based habits**. Instead of only chasing a goal, I should think about who I want to become, because every action I repeat strengthens that identity.

## Question 6
To make a habit easier, I can:

- Make the cue obvious.
- Cut down the number of steps.
- Get everything ready beforehand.
- Start with a very small version.
- Set up my surroundings to support it.

## Question 7
To make a bad habit harder, I can:

- Hide the cue.
- Add friction to the behaviour.
- Remove easy access.
- Put extra steps or barriers in the way.

## Question 8
**Habit to build: Coding practice every day**

- Keep my laptop and study material ready.
- Fix a time for it.
- Start with just 10 minutes on one simple problem.
- Celebrate after each session.

## Question 9
**Habit to reduce: Scrolling social media too much**

- Move the apps off my home screen.
- Turn off notifications.
- Keep my phone away while I study.
- Set app time limits.
- Make coding the easier thing to do.
